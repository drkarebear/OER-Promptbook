Using AI With The OER Promptbook

You do not need to become a prompt engineer. Choose a prompt, add information about your course, paste it into an AI tool, and then verify the results before you use them.

The 30-Second Version

A prompt is simply an instruction you give an AI tool.

The prompts on this site are already written for you. Replace anything in [brackets] with your own course information, then copy and paste the prompt into the AI tool you use.

What Is a Prompt?

A useful prompt tells the AI what you are trying to accomplish, gives it enough context to understand your course, and explains what kind of answer would be useful.

Very broad

A prompt that gives AI little direction

Find OER for psychology.
More useful

A prompt with context and a clear result

I teach introductory psychology at a community college. Help me find OER that covers learning, memory, development, and social psychology. Give me no more than five strong options, identify the open license, and link to the original source.

That is the basic idea. You do not need special vocabulary, formulas, or a perfect first attempt.

How to Use a Prompt From This Site

  1. Choose the task you want help with. For example, find OER, evaluate a resource, fill a gap, or build a course plan.
  2. Choose your prompt level. Use the simple version when you want a quick, manageable result or the power-user version when you want a deeper workflow.
  3. Replace the brackets. Add your course name, topics, outcomes, workload limit, or other requested information.
  4. Paste the prompt into your AI tool. If the prompt asks for a syllabus, COR, PDF, or other file, upload it when your tool supports file analysis.
  5. Review the result. Open the sources, verify important claims, and use your faculty judgment before adopting anything.

Choose the Level That Fits You

Both versions are meant to be copied and pasted. The difference is how much work you are asking the AI to do.

I'm New to AI — Keep It Simple

Use this when you want a short prompt, a focused answer, and only a few strong options.

Good for: getting started, quick searches, one unit, one decision, or anyone who does not want to manage a complicated AI workflow.

I'm a Power User — Go Deeper

Use this when you want AI to work in stages, search again when results are weak, compare evidence, identify gaps, and document uncertainty.

Good for: full-course research, difficult searches, multiple resources, complex comparisons, or major course redesigns.

What AI Tool Can I Use?

The Promptbook is not tied to one company. A general-purpose AI assistant that can follow detailed instructions, work with links, and ideally analyze uploaded files can usually work with these prompts.

Best place to start

Check What Your College Provides

Your college or district may already provide access to an AI tool such as Microsoft Copilot or another institutionally supported service.

An institution-provided account may come with additional privacy, security, accessibility, support, and compliance protections. Ask your IT, library, distance education, or professional development office what is available.

Personal AI accounts

General-Purpose AI Assistants

You can also use a personal account with tools such as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, or another general-purpose AI assistant.

Features differ by product and plan. File uploads, web access, longer context windows, and search capabilities can affect how well a particular prompt works.

Protect Privacy Before You Paste

If you use a personal or public AI account, review its privacy controls first. Where the service offers the option, we recommend turning off the use of your conversations for model improvement or training.

Turning off model training does not make a public AI tool appropriate for confidential information.

Do not paste student names, identifiable student work, grades, accommodations, personnel matters, passwords, protected records, or other confidential college information into a public AI tool. Follow your college or district policies.

ChatGPT Personal Accounts

Look for Settings → Data Controls → Improve the model for everyone and turn that setting off if you do not want new conversations used to improve OpenAI's models.

AI Can Be Wrong. Verify Before You Use the Result.

AI can save search and comparison time, but it can also misunderstand a source, give you an outdated link, or confidently invent information.

  • Open the original source. Do not rely only on the AI summary.
  • Verify the license. A resource that is free to read online is not automatically OER.
  • Check accessibility yourself. AI should not be treated as an accessibility certifier.
  • Verify library access locally. Another institution's ebook license does not mean your students have access.
  • Use your academic judgment. You decide whether the content is accurate, appropriate, current, and worth assigning.

Why We Don't Say “Use AI for Everything”

Generative AI can save meaningful faculty time, but it also raises environmental, labor, bias, privacy, and intellectual-property concerns. The OER Promptbook encourages purposeful rather than automatic AI use.
