Decide Whether an OER Is Worth Your Time

You do not need to read every page before deciding whether a resource deserves a closer look. Use AI for a structured first pass, then focus your own review on the strongest candidates and the issues that matter most for your course.

  • ✓ Check course fit
  • ✓ Spot gaps and red flags
  • ✓ Focus your faculty review

AI Can Help You Screen an OER. It Cannot Approve It for You.

These prompts are designed to help you decide what deserves your attention next. Upload the resource when your AI tool allows file analysis, or provide a direct link if the tool can access the web.

Important: Ask AI to distinguish what it can verify from what it cannot. Accessibility, accuracy, licensing, and course fit still need human review.

What Do You Want to Check?

👀 Is This Worth a Closer Look? Get a manageable first-pass evaluation of one OER. 🎯 Does It Fit My Course? Compare the resource with your COR, outcomes, or syllabus. 🔓 Check License and Accessibility Flag what appears verified and what still needs manual review. 🧭 Find Gaps and Weak Spots Look for missing content, dated material, and uneven coverage.
Evaluation 1

Is This OER Worth a Closer Look?

Use this before you spend hours reading. The goal is not a final verdict. It is a focused screening that tells you where your own attention is most needed.

🌱 Quick Prompt — Keep It Simple

Best when you want a quick overview without a giant rubric.

Keep this simple and focused.

I am considering this Open Educational Resource for a community college course:

[UPLOAD THE RESOURCE, PROVIDE THE LINK, OR PASTE THE RELEVANT INFORMATION]

My course is:
[COURSE NAME AND SHORT DESCRIPTION]

Give me a brief first-pass evaluation to help me decide whether this resource is worth reviewing more closely.

Tell me:
- What the resource appears to cover well
- What level or audience it seems designed for
- Its biggest strengths for my course
- Any obvious gaps, weaknesses, or concerns
- Whether you can verify an open license
- What I should personally check before adopting it

Do not invent information that is not in the resource or source page.

Clearly separate:
1. What you can verify
2. What you are inferring
3. What still needs human review

End with one of these:
- Strong candidate for closer review
- Possible candidate with important questions
- Probably not the best fit

Explain that recommendation in 2–3 sentences.
🚀 Deeper Workflow — Evaluate More Deeply

Best when you want a systematic screening before conducting your own close review.

Conduct a structured first-pass evaluation of the OER below to help me decide whether it deserves a full faculty review.

RESOURCE
[UPLOAD THE RESOURCE, PROVIDE THE LINK, OR PASTE THE RELEVANT CONTENT]

COURSE CONTEXT
[COURSE NAME, LEVEL, DESCRIPTION, OUTCOMES, OR OTHER RELEVANT CONTEXT]

EVALUATION AREAS
Assess only what the available evidence supports:

1. Scope and coverage
2. Depth and likely academic level
3. Organization and ease of assigning sections
4. Clarity and instructional usability
5. Accuracy or currency concerns that are visible from the material
6. Availability of examples, exercises, activities, or instructor supports
7. Open-license information
8. Accessibility information that is explicitly documented
9. Major strengths
10. Major gaps or concerns

EVIDENCE RULES
- Cite or point to specific sections, pages, headings, or source information when possible.
- Do not invent features, ancillaries, accessibility claims, or license terms.
- Distinguish verified facts from reasonable inference.
- Mark anything that requires manual review.
- Do not treat "free online" as proof that the resource is OER.

OUTPUT
Create a concise table:
Area | What the Evidence Shows | Concern or Strength | Needs Human Review?

Then give me:
- The 3 strongest reasons to keep reviewing this resource
- The 3 most important questions or concerns
- The sections I should personally inspect first
- A provisional recommendation: Strong candidate / Possible candidate / Weak candidate

Treat this as screening, not a final adoption decision.
Want a faster review?

Add: “Limit the first pass to the five issues most likely to affect an adoption decision. I can ask for a deeper review afterward.”

Evaluation 2

Does This OER Fit My Course?

Use this when the resource looks promising, but you need to know how well it actually matches your course before investing more time.

🌱 Quick Prompt — Keep It Simple

Best when you have a course description, outcomes, or weekly topics you can paste.

Keep this simple and focused.

I am considering this OER:
[UPLOAD OR LINK TO THE OER]

Here is information about my course:
[PASTE COURSE DESCRIPTION, LEARNING OUTCOMES, OR MAIN TOPICS]

Compare the OER with my course.

Create a simple table with:
Course Need | Covered Well | Partly Covered | Missing or Unclear

Then tell me:
- The strongest matches
- The biggest gaps
- Whether the OER seems better as a primary resource or a supplemental resource
- Which parts of the OER I should review first

Do not assume coverage that you cannot verify.

Do not invent course requirements or OER content.

Keep the answer focused on helping me decide whether this resource is worth my time.
🚀 Deeper Workflow — Map the Alignment

Best when you want a detailed coverage map grounded in your course documents.

Evaluate how well this OER aligns with my community college course.

OER
[UPLOAD OR LINK TO THE RESOURCE]

COURSE MATERIALS
[PASTE COR LANGUAGE, COURSE DESCRIPTION, OUTCOMES, OBJECTIVES, TOPICS, SYLLABUS, OR WEEKLY SCHEDULE]

PHASE 1: COURSE-NEEDS MAP
Extract the major content, skills, and recurring practices that are explicitly supported by my course materials.

Do not invent requirements.

PHASE 2: ALIGNMENT MAP
For each major course need, identify whether the OER:
- covers it strongly
- covers it partially
- appears to omit it
- cannot be assessed from the available evidence

Where possible, point to the specific chapter, section, page, or feature that supports your assessment.

PHASE 3: ADOPTION FIT
Assess whether the OER is more suitable as:
- a primary course resource
- one major component of a multi-resource course
- a supplemental resource
- a poor fit

OUTPUT
Create a table:
Course Need | OER Evidence | Alignment | Important Notes

Then provide:
1. Overall estimated coverage in plain language, not a fake precision score.
2. The strongest areas of alignment.
3. The most consequential gaps.
4. Parts of my existing course that would likely need replacement, supplementation, or redesign.
5. The 3 sections of the OER I should inspect personally before making a decision.

Do not claim alignment unless you can point to evidence.
Evaluation 3

Check Licensing, Accessibility, and Practical Usability

Use this to flag issues that are easy to overlook. AI can help surface evidence, but this prompt deliberately tells it not to certify accessibility or licensing when the source does not support that conclusion.

🌱 Quick Prompt — Keep It Simple

Best when you want a short checklist of what looks good and what still needs verification.

Keep this simple and focused.

Please review this OER or its source page:
[UPLOAD OR LINK]

Help me check three things:

1. LICENSE
Can you verify the specific open license or public-domain status?
What does that license appear to allow me to do?

2. ACCESSIBILITY
What accessibility information is explicitly documented?
Do not assume the resource is accessible if there is no evidence.

3. PRACTICAL USE
Can students access it without paying?
Is it easy to navigate and assign by section?
Are download or format options clearly available?

For each area, label the result:
- Verified
- Appears promising but needs checking
- Not enough information

Do not invent license terms, accessibility features, or access conditions.

End with a short "Check This Yourself" list of anything I should verify manually.
🚀 Deeper Workflow — Audit the Evidence

Best when licensing, remix rights, accessibility, and technical usability may affect adoption.

Conduct an evidence-based licensing, accessibility, and usability review of this OER.

RESOURCE
[UPLOAD OR LINK]

LICENSE REVIEW
- Identify the exact license from the original source.
- State the license version when available.
- Summarize what the license permits regarding reuse, revision, remixing, redistribution, and commercial use.
- Identify any attribution, ShareAlike, or NonCommercial conditions that are explicitly present.
- Do not infer a license from a repository label if the resource itself provides conflicting or incomplete information.

ACCESSIBILITY REVIEW
Report only accessibility evidence that is actually available, such as:
- accessibility statement or VPAT/ACR
- tagged or structured document information
- heading structure
- alt text evidence
- captions or transcripts
- keyboard/navigation information
- accessible file-format options
- known barriers visible in the resource

Do not certify WCAG or ADA compliance unless an authoritative source explicitly establishes it.

PRACTICAL-USABILITY REVIEW
Check:
- whether student access appears free
- sign-in or account requirements
- download options
- print/PDF/EPUB/HTML availability
- ease of linking to chapters or sections
- visible update/version information
- whether ancillaries are openly available or separately restricted

OUTPUT
Create a table:
Area | Verified Evidence | Concern | What I Still Need to Check

Then provide:
1. License summary for faculty use.
2. Accessibility questions requiring manual review.
3. Student-access or usability concerns.
4. Any issue serious enough that I should resolve it before adoption.

Be conservative. "Not verified" is better than guessing.
Evaluation 4

Find Gaps, Dated Material, and Uneven Coverage

Use this when an OER is almost right and you need to know what you would still have to supplement, update, or teach around.

🌱 Quick Prompt — Keep It Simple

Best when you want a focused list of the places most likely to create extra prep work.

Keep this simple and focused.

Review this OER:
[UPLOAD OR LINK]

My course is:
[COURSE NAME AND SHORT DESCRIPTION]

Help me identify the parts that may create extra work for me.

Look for:
- important topics that seem missing
- sections that may be too shallow or too advanced
- examples or data that may be dated
- areas where students may need more explanation or practice
- perspectives, examples, or contexts that seem limited or uneven
- places where I may need another reading, activity, or resource

For each concern, point to the evidence you are using.

Do not invent problems just to fill the list.

End with:
- "Probably fine as is"
- "Needs a few supplements"
- "Would require substantial rebuilding"

Explain why in a few sentences.
🚀 Deeper Workflow — Diagnose the Gaps

Best when you want to understand how much adoption work the resource would actually create.

Diagnose the likely adoption work required by this OER.

OER
[UPLOAD OR LINK]

COURSE CONTEXT
[COURSE DESCRIPTION, OUTCOMES, TOPICS, OR SYLLABUS]

Evaluate the resource for:

CONTENT GAPS
- required or important course topics that appear absent
- areas with insufficient depth
- areas that substantially exceed the likely course level

CURRENCY
- dated examples, statistics, links, technology, policies, terminology, or research where currency matters
- distinguish content that is merely old from content that is actually outdated

LEARNING SUPPORT
- places where students may need more explanation, examples, practice, scaffolding, or application
- missing ancillaries or exercises when they materially affect usability

RANGE OF PERSPECTIVES
- note whether examples, authors, cases, populations, or contexts appear narrow or uneven when that matters to the discipline
- describe evidence rather than making unsupported claims about representation

ADOPTION LABOR
Estimate the kind of work I would need to do:
- use as is
- light supplementation
- moderate adaptation
- substantial rebuilding

OUTPUT
Create a table:
Issue | Evidence | Why It Matters | Likely Faculty Work | Possible Fix

Then identify:
1. The 5 highest-priority issues.
2. Which issues can likely be solved with one supplemental OER.
3. Which issues would require instructor-created material or deeper redesign.
4. Whether the resource still appears worth adopting given the likely workload.

Do not manufacture deficiencies. If the evidence is insufficient, say so.

Before You Adopt

An AI evaluation is a screening tool, not a substitute for faculty review. Use it to decide where to spend your attention.

  • Read representative sections yourself. Check accuracy, tone, depth, and whether the material works for your students.
  • Verify the license at the original source. Do not rely only on an AI summary.
  • Check accessibility directly. Automated or AI review can miss barriers.
  • Look at the student experience. Open the resource the way a student would and test navigation, downloads, links, and formats.
  • Be realistic about adoption labor. A resource that is "free" may still cost you a great deal of redesign time.

What Next?

Good resource with a few holes? Fill the gaps. Ready to use it? Map it into your course. Not a fit? Go back and find stronger OER options.
