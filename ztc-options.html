When OER isn't enough

Explore Other Ways to Get Students to $0

A course can be zero-textbook-cost without relying only on OER. Use AI to identify promising library ebooks, streaming media, databases, and other no-cost-to-student options, then ask your librarian to verify what your college can actually provide.

  • ✓ Find possibilities faster
  • ✓ Bring your librarian a focused shortlist
  • ✓ Keep access at $0 for students

AI Finds Possibilities. Your Librarian Verifies Access.

Institutional ebook pricing and licenses are often not public, and the same title may be sold under different access models. Use these prompts to find strong candidates, not to guess what your library can buy.

Do not use Amazon, Kindle, or other consumer prices as evidence of library cost or availability.

For California Community Colleges

California ZTC guidance recognizes that library-provided materials can support zero-textbook-cost courses when students have appropriate no-cost access. Licensing and sustainability still matter, so your librarian is an essential partner.

ZTC 1

Find Library eBooks to Ask Your Librarian About

Use this when you are open to more than one title and want AI to find commercially published books that may serve the same instructional purpose.

🌱 Quick Prompt — Keep It Simple

Best when you want a short librarian-ready list.

Keep this simple and focused.

I teach [COURSE] at a community college.

I need students to read about:
[TOPIC, UNIT, OR TYPE OF BOOK]

Help me find commercially published books that may be worth asking my college librarian about as possible Zero Textbook Cost (ZTC) ebooks.

Prioritize:
- books suitable for college students
- books from academic, university, independent, or reputable trade publishers
- books that appear in academic-library ebook channels or collections
- books that could replace a meaningful amount of course reading

Give me no more than 5 strong candidates.

For each one, provide:
- Title and author
- Publisher and year
- Why it fits my course
- Evidence that it may be available through an academic-library ebook platform, if you can verify that
- What my librarian still needs to check

Tell my librarian to verify:
- institutional availability
- current institutional price
- simultaneous-user or unlimited-user access
- perpetual purchase or subscription terms
- DRM or download restrictions
- accessibility
- whether my library already owns it

Do not use consumer ebook prices as a substitute for institutional library pricing.

Do not claim my library can buy or license a title unless that has been verified by my library.
🚀 Deeper Workflow — Search More Strategically

Best when you want California precedents, multiple alternatives, and a more evidence-based shortlist.

Conduct an evidence-based search for commercially published ebooks that could support a Zero Textbook Cost (ZTC) version of this community college course.

COURSE
[COURSE NAME, LEVEL, TOPICS, OUTCOMES, OR CURRENT READING NEED]

SEARCH PRIORITIES
1. Look for books already documented as library-supported ZTC at colleges, especially California community colleges when evidence is available.
2. Search for titles associated with academic-library ebook platforms or publishers commonly represented in academic libraries.
3. If my exact topic or preferred book appears difficult to license, identify alternatives that serve the same instructional purpose.
4. Favor books that could cover a substantial unit or multiple weeks rather than a very narrow topic.

POSSIBLE LIBRARY CHANNELS
Look for public evidence of availability through platforms such as EBSCO eBooks, ProQuest Ebook Central, OverDrive/Libby, or other academic-library channels when relevant.

Do not assume platform evidence means my institution has access.

VERIFICATION RULES
- Do not use consumer retail prices as institutional prices.
- Do not invent unlimited-user availability, institutional cost, or license terms.
- Distinguish confirmed library-supported ZTC at another institution from a merely plausible candidate.
- Link to credible evidence when available.
- Label uncertainty clearly.

OUTPUT
Create a table:
Title | Author | Publisher | Course Use | Library/ZTC Evidence | Why It Is Promising | What Librarian Must Verify

Then:
1. Rank the 5 strongest titles to send to my librarian first.
2. Put titles already documented as library-supported ZTC at another college near the top.
3. Identify any title that seems unlikely to work because of access-model concerns.
4. Suggest alternative search terms or publishers if the results are weak.
Already have a current textbook?

Add: “I currently use [TITLE]. Find books that serve a similar instructional purpose. Do not assume I need an exact one-to-one replacement.”

ZTC 2

Investigate a Specific Book Before You Email the Library

Use this when you already know the book you want. The prompt gathers public evidence and turns it into a concise question for your librarian.

🌱 Quick Prompt — Keep It Simple

Best when you want to know whether a title is worth sending to your librarian.

Keep this simple and focused.

I would like to use this book in a community college course:

Title: [TITLE]
Author: [AUTHOR]
Publisher or ISBN: [OPTIONAL]

Find reliable public evidence that the book may be available as an institutional library ebook.

Look for:
- publisher information
- academic-library ebook platform listings or references
- evidence that another college library provides the book
- evidence that another college uses it as ZTC, if available

Do not use Amazon, Kindle, or other consumer ebook listings as proof of library availability.

Do not guess the institutional price or number of users.

At the end, give me:
1. A short summary of what you found.
2. The evidence links.
3. A brief message I can send my librarian asking them to check institutional availability, access model, price, accessibility, and whether my library already owns it.
🚀 Deeper Workflow — Build a Librarian Brief

Best when you want the public evidence organized before asking the library to investigate.

Build a concise librarian-ready research brief for this title:

TITLE
[TITLE, AUTHOR, PUBLISHER, ISBN IF AVAILABLE]

COURSE USE
[HOW I PLAN TO USE THE BOOK AND APPROXIMATE ENROLLMENT IF USEFUL]

PUBLIC RESEARCH
Search for reliable evidence related to:
- publisher ebook availability
- academic-library ebook channels
- institutional library catalog records
- documented ZTC or course use at other colleges
- available access-model language if it is genuinely public

Do not infer institutional pricing from consumer pricing.

Do not claim unlimited-user, DRM-free, perpetual, or other access terms unless directly supported by evidence.

OUTPUT
Create these sections:

PUBLIC EVIDENCE
Source | What It Shows | Link

WHY I AM ASKING
Briefly explain the instructional role the title would play.

QUESTIONS FOR MY LIBRARIAN
Ask them to verify:
- whether the title is available from my library's vendors
- institutional price
- user/access model
- perpetual vs. subscription terms
- DRM/download restrictions
- accessibility
- whether the college already owns or subscribes to it
- whether a similar alternative has a better access model

DRAFT EMAIL
Write a short, collegial email to my librarian with the title, course use, and verification questions.

Keep the brief concise and do not overstate what the public evidence proves.
ZTC 3

Find Other Library-Supported ZTC Materials

Sometimes the best answer is not another book. Your library may already provide streaming films, articles, databases, reference works, primary sources, or other digital materials that can replace paid course content.

🌱 Quick Prompt — Keep It Simple

Best when you want a few categories to investigate with your librarian.

Keep this simple and focused.

I teach [COURSE].

Students need materials for:
[TOPIC, UNIT, OR ASSIGNMENT]

Help me think beyond textbooks.

Find promising possibilities such as:
- library ebooks
- streaming films or documentaries
- journal or magazine articles
- databases
- reference collections
- primary-source collections
- audiobooks
- other digital resources commonly licensed by academic libraries

Give me 5–8 strong possibilities or resource types.

For each one, explain:
- what it could contribute to the course
- why it might work as a ZTC resource
- what I should ask my librarian to check

Do not assume my college subscribes to a database or owns a resource.

Clearly separate:
- something that appears to be a specific library resource
- a database or collection my library may subscribe to
- a general idea that still needs librarian research

End with the 3 questions I should take to my librarian first.
🚀 Deeper Workflow — Build a Multi-Format Search

Best when one format is not enough and you want a richer ZTC mix.

Build a multi-format ZTC discovery plan for this community college course.

COURSE NEED
[COURSE, UNIT, OUTCOMES, OR PAID MATERIAL I WANT TO REPLACE]

Search for possibilities across:
- library ebooks
- streaming media
- periodical and article databases
- reference resources
- primary-source databases
- government or institutional collections
- audiobook collections
- other library-supported digital formats

Where possible, identify California community college or academic-library examples of these resources being used for ZTC.

FOR EACH CANDIDATE OR COLLECTION
Identify:
- instructional purpose
- format
- likely student use
- evidence of academic-library availability
- whether access appears institution-dependent
- what the librarian must verify

Do not claim my library subscribes to anything without evidence from my institution.

OUTPUT
Create a table:
Resource/Collection | Format | Course Use | Evidence | Institution-Dependent? | Librarian Check

Then:
1. Recommend the strongest mix of 3–5 resource types.
2. Identify which materials could replace paid readings rather than merely supplement them.
3. Flag any option likely to create unstable or individual-user access.
4. Draft a concise librarian request focused on the highest-value possibilities.
ZTC 4

Build a ZTC Plan When No Single Resource Works

Use this when the best solution is probably a mix: an OER textbook for the foundation, a library ebook for one unit, a few open readings, and perhaps streaming media or database content.

🌱 Quick Prompt — Keep It Simple

Best when you want a simple replacement plan instead of continuing to hunt for one perfect textbook.

Keep this simple and focused.

I teach [COURSE].

Here is what students need to cover:
[PASTE MAIN UNITS, TOPICS, OR CURRENT TEXTBOOK CONTENTS]

Here are resources I already have:
[PASTE OER, LIBRARY RESOURCES, OR OTHER MATERIALS]

Help me create a Zero Textbook Cost plan using a small mix of materials.

Prioritize:
1. OER and public-domain resources
2. library-provided resources students may be able to access at no cost
3. as few separate resources as possible

Create a table:
Unit | Best Resource | Type | Why It Fits | What Still Needs Verification

Do not assume library access is available just because a resource exists.

Keep the plan simple and avoid giving students a confusing pile of links.

At the end, identify:
- what is already solved
- what needs librarian verification
- what still needs another OER or resource search
🚀 Deeper Workflow — Design a Sustainable ZTC Mix

Best when you want to balance course fit, student simplicity, and access risk.

Design a sustainable multi-resource ZTC plan for this community college course.

COURSE REQUIREMENTS
[PASTE TOPICS, OUTCOMES, WEEKLY PLAN, OR CURRENT MATERIALS]

AVAILABLE OR CANDIDATE RESOURCES
[PASTE OER, PUBLIC-DOMAIN MATERIALS, LIBRARY EBOOK CANDIDATES, DATABASES, STREAMING MEDIA, ETC.]

DESIGN PRIORITIES
- $0 required cost to students
- strong instructional fit
- manageable student navigation
- sustainable access across future terms
- minimal unnecessary faculty rebuilding
- minimal number of separate platforms/resources
- preference for OER when it works well
- library-supported content when it solves a gap

CLASSIFY EACH RESOURCE AS
- Verified OER/public domain
- Confirmed library access at my institution
- Possible library candidate — librarian verification needed
- Unverified suggestion

OUTPUT
Create a table:
Unit | Resource | Classification | Student Access | Strength | Risk/Gap | Next Action

Then provide:
1. The simplest viable ZTC configuration.
2. A more robust backup configuration if a library license becomes unavailable.
3. Any single point of failure in the plan.
4. Resources that create too much complexity for the value they add.
5. A short librarian checklist for every institution-dependent item.
6. A short faculty checklist for every OER item.

Do not treat another institution's ownership or license as proof that my students can access the resource.

Before You Call It ZTC

AI can help identify promising materials, but your local access conditions determine whether students truly have a zero-cost option.

  • Ask your librarian to verify institutional access. Another college's license does not apply to yours.
  • Check the user model. One-user or limited-user ebooks may not work for an entire class.
  • Check sustainability. Subscription access can change, and perpetual access may have different costs or restrictions.
  • Check accessibility and student usability. Make sure students can actually use the platform and format.
  • Keep a backup. For a critical library resource, identify an OER or alternate option in case access changes.
