Advanced AI Workflows for Harder OER Problems

Use these multi-step workflows when a straightforward prompt is not enough. They help AI work in stages, check evidence, refine weak searches, and turn successful processes into reusable tools.

  • ✓ Run deeper research
  • ✓ Iterate instead of starting over
  • ✓ Turn what works into reusable prompts

Advanced Workflows Are About Process, Not Prompt Length

A strong advanced workflow may ask AI to analyze your course, search, verify, compare, identify gaps, run a second search, and then report what still needs human judgment.

You do not need an advanced workflow just because you are experienced with AI. Use one when the task itself requires multiple stages, verification, iteration, or reusable process design. For straightforward tasks, the shorter prompts elsewhere in The OER Promptbook are usually the better choice.

What Advanced Workflow Do You Need?

🔬 Deep OER Research Move from course requirements to a verified, ranked resource strategy. ↻ Improve a Weak Search Diagnose why the first results were poor and search again deliberately. ✎ Improve My Prompt Use AI to revise a prompt while preserving your actual teaching goal. ▣ Make a Reusable Template Turn a successful conversation into a prompt you or colleagues can reuse.
Advanced Workflow 1

Run a Deep OER Research Workflow

Use this when you want a serious search from course needs through shortlist, verification, gaps, and next steps.

🚀 Copy-and-Paste Advanced Workflow

Best for a new OER adoption, a course redesign, or a discipline where good resources are hard to locate.

Act as a research assistant helping a community college faculty member investigate OER and other zero-cost course materials.

COURSE
[COURSE NAME AND NUMBER]

COURSE MATERIALS
[PASTE OR UPLOAD THE COR, COURSE DESCRIPTION, OUTCOMES, TOPICS, SYLLABUS, CURRENT TEXTBOOK TABLE OF CONTENTS, OR OTHER RELEVANT MATERIAL]

GOAL
[DESCRIBE WHAT I AM TRYING TO REPLACE, FIND, OR REDESIGN]

WORK IN STAGES.

STAGE 1 — UNDERSTAND THE COURSE
Create a concise course-needs map based only on the materials I provide.
Separate:
- essential content
- important skills or practices
- major applications or assignments, if provided
- optional or instructor-specific content

Do not invent requirements.

STAGE 2 — SEARCH
Search multiple credible OER sources and original resource pages.

Look for:
- substantial OER that could cover much of the course
- modular OER for important gaps
- primary sources, media, or activities when useful

Use alternate terminology and related disciplines when the obvious search terms are weak.

STAGE 3 — VERIFY
For every serious candidate:
- confirm that the resource exists
- link to the original source
- identify the open license or public-domain status when possible
- distinguish OER from material that is merely free to access
- label anything you cannot verify

STAGE 4 — COMPARE
Compare the strongest candidates for:
- course coverage
- level and depth
- organization
- currency where relevant
- accessibility information that is actually documented
- activities or ancillaries
- faculty adaptation work
- likely student workload

Do not create fake numerical precision.

STAGE 5 — SEARCH AGAIN
Identify the 3 most important remaining gaps.

For each gap, run a second focused search using:
- alternate terminology
- narrower resource types
- related disciplines
- individual chapters or readings rather than only complete textbooks

Do not conclude that no useful OER exists until you have tried a meaningfully different second search.

STAGE 6 — REPORT
Give me:

1. COURSE NEEDS AT A GLANCE
2. TOP 5–8 RESOURCES
Resource | Best Use | Strength | Gap | License | Direct Link | Verification Notes
3. STRONGEST 3 OPTIONS AND TRADEOFFS
4. REMAINING GAPS
5. WHAT I SHOULD PERSONALLY REVIEW FIRST
6. NEXT ACTIONS

Keep the final report decision-focused. I want a research strategy and shortlist, not a giant catalog.
Why this is different:

The prompt separates the work into stages and requires a second search after the first pass. That makes it harder for the AI to treat its first five search results as the end of the research process.

Advanced Workflow 2

Diagnose and Improve a Weak OER Search

Use this after an AI search gives you generic textbooks, dead links, poor course matches, or too many resources that are free but not actually OER.

↻ Copy-and-Paste Search Repair Prompt

Paste the weak results or describe what went wrong. The AI should diagnose one problem at a time instead of randomly making the prompt longer.

Help me improve an OER search that did not produce useful results.

ORIGINAL GOAL
[PASTE MY ORIGINAL PROMPT OR DESCRIBE WHAT I NEEDED]

RESULTS I GOT
[PASTE THE RESULTS OR SUMMARIZE THEM]

WHAT WAS WRONG
[OPTIONAL: TOO GENERIC / WRONG COURSE LEVEL / DEAD LINKS / NOT REALLY OER / POOR COVERAGE / TOO MANY RESULTS / OTHER]

Do not immediately run another broad search.

STEP 1 — DIAGNOSE
Identify the 1–3 most likely reasons the search underperformed.

Look for problems such as:
- search terms that were too broad
- discipline terminology that was too narrow
- searching only for full textbooks
- failure to search related disciplines
- confusing "free" with openly licensed
- insufficient course context
- output instructions that encouraged quantity over quality

STEP 2 — REVISE
Propose the smallest meaningful changes to the search strategy.

Explain each change briefly.

STEP 3 — SEARCH AGAIN
Run a new search using the revised strategy.

Change one major dimension at a time when possible so I can tell what improved the results.

STEP 4 — COMPARE
Show:
Original problem | Change made | What improved | What still did not work

STEP 5 — NEXT ITERATION
If the second search is still weak, recommend the single most useful next change rather than rewriting everything again.

Do not invent resources or licensing information to make the revised search look successful.
Useful habit:

When a search fails, change something deliberately and see what happens. Avoid adding five new instructions at once, because you will not know which change actually helped.

Advanced Workflow 3

Ask AI to Improve Your Prompt

You do not have to manually engineer every prompt. Give AI your rough version and ask it to strengthen only the parts that will materially improve the result.

✎ Copy-and-Paste Metaprompt

This is especially useful when you know your teaching goal but are not sure how much context or structure the AI needs.

Act as a prompt editor.

I am a college faculty member using AI to help with OER or Zero Textbook Cost course materials.

Here is my draft prompt:

[PASTE MY DRAFT PROMPT]

MY ACTUAL GOAL
[OPTIONAL: EXPLAIN WHAT I AM TRYING TO ACCOMPLISH]

Improve the prompt without changing my teaching goal.

Use these principles:
- make the task clear
- add course context only when it will improve the result
- make important constraints explicit
- specify a useful output format
- include verification instructions when the task involves OER, licenses, links, accessibility, library access, or other factual claims
- reduce ambiguity
- remove unnecessary repetition
- do not make the prompt longer just to make it look more advanced

If important information is missing, ask me no more than 3 questions, and only if the answers would materially change the prompt.

Then give me:

1. REVISED PROMPT
Ready to copy and paste.

2. WHAT YOU CHANGED
Briefly explain the 3–5 most important improvements.

3. OPTIONAL POWER-UP
Suggest one additional instruction I could add if I want a deeper result.

Do not execute the revised prompt unless I ask you to.
Advanced Workflow 4

Turn a Successful Conversation Into a Reusable Prompt

Use this after you get a genuinely useful result. Instead of rebuilding the workflow next semester, ask AI to extract what worked and turn it into a clean template.

▣ Copy-and-Paste Template Builder

Use this in the same conversation where the AI has already helped you successfully complete an OER/ZTC task.

The workflow in this conversation produced a useful result.

Help me turn what worked into a reusable prompt template that I or another college faculty member could use again.

STEP 1 — IDENTIFY WHAT MATTERED
Review the conversation and identify:
- the actual task
- the context that materially improved the result
- important constraints
- verification rules
- the output structure that was useful
- follow-up or second-search steps that improved the answer

Ignore conversational detours and instructions that did not materially help.

STEP 2 — BUILD THE TEMPLATE
Create one reusable copy-and-paste prompt.

Replace course-specific information with clear placeholders such as:
[COURSE]
[TOPIC]
[LEARNING OUTCOMES]
[RESOURCE]
[WORKLOAD LIMIT]

The prompt should be understandable without seeing this conversation.

STEP 3 — MAKE IT ROBUST
Include only safeguards relevant to the task, such as:
- do not invent sources or links
- distinguish free access from OER
- verify licensing when possible
- label uncertainty
- distinguish institution-specific library access from general availability

STEP 4 — DOCUMENT IT
After the prompt, provide:

PURPOSE
One sentence explaining what the prompt is for.

WHAT TO PASTE
A short list of what the user should prepare.

GOOD OUTPUT SHOULD
List 3–5 qualities of a successful result.

WARNING SIGNS
List 3–5 signs that the AI output needs another pass.

Keep the final prompt reusable and focused. Do not preserve unnecessary complexity from the original conversation.

Advanced AI Habits That Matter More Than Fancy Prompting

  • Start with a clear goal. More instructions cannot rescue a fuzzy task.
  • Iterate deliberately. When something fails, diagnose the failure and change the part most likely to matter.
  • Use AI to improve prompts. You do not have to manually guess which wording would work better.
  • Save what works. A successful prompt is more valuable when it becomes a reusable template.
  • Keep an evidence boundary. Ask the AI to separate what it verified, what it inferred, and what still needs human review.
  • Stop when the task is solved. A longer workflow is not automatically a better one.
