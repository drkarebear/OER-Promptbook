Diagnose before you add

Your Course Almost Works. Fix the Missing Piece.

If a course or unit is mostly working, start by identifying what is actually missing. Then use AI to find the smallest useful addition: practice, media, a primary source, context, perspective, or a targeted reading.

  • ✓ Identify the real gap
  • ✓ Add only what earns its place
  • ✓ Protect student workload

Start by Diagnosing the Gap

Sometimes a weak week needs another resource. Sometimes it needs a clearer example, more practice, a different kind of source, or simply less repetition. The first prompt below helps you identify the instructional need before you start searching.

Tip: Describe what students need to do, where they are getting stuck, and what they already have. The goal is not to add more material. It is to find the smallest change that makes the unit work better.

Figure Out What This Unit Is Missing

Use this before searching when a unit feels weak but you are not sure another reading is actually the answer. AI can help name the instructional gap and recommend the smallest useful next step.

🌱 Quick Prompt — Keep It Simple

Best when you know something is not working but are not sure what kind of resource would help.

Keep this simple and focused.

I teach [COURSE] at a community college.

This unit or week is about:
[TOPIC]

Students should be able to:
[LEARNING GOAL]

What students already have:
[READINGS, VIDEOS, LECTURES, ACTIVITIES, OR OTHER MATERIALS]

What seems weak or where students tend to get stuck:
[DESCRIBE THE PROBLEM]

Before suggesting more material, help me diagnose what this unit actually needs.

Consider whether the main gap is:
- explanation or content
- an example or model
- practice or application
- context or a different perspective
- evidence, data, media, or a primary source
- accessibility or format
- updated information
- unnecessary repetition or too much material
- something else

Tell me:
1. The 1–2 most likely gaps.
2. Why you think those are the gaps based on what I provided.
3. Whether I actually need another resource.
4. The smallest change that would most likely improve the unit.

If a new resource would help, describe exactly what kind of resource I should look for and what students should do with it.

Do not search for resources yet.

End with one clear next step: find a reading, find practice, find media or a primary source, add perspective/context, revise what I already have, or add nothing.
🚀 Deeper Workflow — Diagnose Before Searching

Best when you want AI to distinguish a real instructional gap from a resource problem, workload problem, or design problem.

Diagnose this unit before recommending that I add another resource.

COURSE
[COURSE NAME AND LEVEL]

UNIT OR WEEK
[TOPIC]

LEARNING GOAL
Students should be able to:
[LEARNING GOAL]

CURRENT MATERIALS
[PASTE TITLES, LINKS, SHORT DESCRIPTIONS, ASSIGNMENTS, OR A UNIT OUTLINE]

WHAT I AM NOTICING
[CONFUSION, WEAK TRANSFER, LACK OF PRACTICE, OUTDATED EXAMPLES, NARROW CONTEXT, LOW ENGAGEMENT, WORKLOAD, OR OTHER CONCERN]

PHASE 1: INVENTORY
Briefly identify what instructional functions the current materials already serve. For example: introduce content, model a skill, provide evidence, offer practice, add context, or assess learning.

PHASE 2: DIAGNOSE
Identify the strongest evidence for any meaningful gap. Consider:
- missing explanation or foundational content
- missing model or worked example
- insufficient practice or application
- missing context or perspective
- missing primary source, evidence, data, or media
- accessibility or format barriers
- currency or outdated information
- redundancy or excess workload
- unclear alignment between the materials and the learning goal

Do not assume that more content is the solution.

PHASE 3: RECOMMEND THE SMALLEST INTERVENTION
Rank up to 3 possible interventions from highest to lowest value. These may include revising, replacing, shortening, reordering, making something optional, adding practice, or adding one targeted resource.

PHASE 4: CREATE A SEARCH BRIEF ONLY IF NEEDED
If a new resource is justified, give me a concise search brief with:
Resource type | Instructional purpose | What it must add | What it should not duplicate | Suggested maximum student time

Do not search for resources yet.

End with the best next step and point me toward one category: reading, practice/activity, media/primary source, perspective/context, revise existing materials, or no new resource.

Gap Search 1

Find Practice, an Activity, or an Ancillary

Use this when the content is there, but students need something to do with it: practice, discussion, application, self-checks, or other learning support.

🌱 Quick Prompt — Keep It Simple

Best when you want to find an existing open activity before creating one yourself.

Keep this simple and focused.

I teach [COURSE] at a community college.

Students are learning:
[TOPIC OR SKILL]

I already have:
[READING, CHAPTER, VIDEO, LECTURE, OR OTHER MATERIAL]

I need an open resource that gives students practice with:
[WHAT STUDENTS SHOULD DO]

Look for existing OER such as:
- activities
- exercises
- discussion prompts
- worksheets
- low-stakes practice
- case studies
- simulations
- quizzes or self-checks

Find 3–5 strong options.

For each one, tell me:
- What the activity is
- What students would do
- How long it might take
- Why it fits my goal
- Open license
- Direct link

Do not invent an activity and present it as an existing OER.

If you cannot find a strong existing OER, clearly say so instead of pretending one exists.
🚀 Deeper Workflow — Search and Diagnose

Best when you want AI to distinguish between existing OER and something it could help you create later.

Search for existing openly licensed learning activities or ancillaries that fill this instructional gap.

COURSE
[COURSE]

CONTENT STUDENTS ALREADY HAVE
[READING, TEXTBOOK CHAPTER, VIDEO, OR DESCRIPTION]

TARGET SKILL OR LEARNING GOAL
[WHAT STUDENTS NEED TO PRACTICE]

PREFERRED ACTIVITY
[DISCUSSION / PRACTICE / APPLICATION / CASE / QUIZ / SELF-CHECK / WORKSHEET / OPEN]

CONSTRAINTS
- Approximate student time: [OPTIONAL]
- Individual or collaborative: [OPTIONAL]
- Online, in-person, or either: [OPTIONAL]
- Low-stakes or graded: [OPTIONAL]

SEARCH RULES
1. Search for existing OER first.
2. Prioritize activities with a clearly stated open license.
3. Include instructor resources or ancillary collections when they are openly available.
4. Do not present AI-generated ideas as if they were existing OER.
5. If strong existing resources are unavailable, separate that finding from any later suggestion to create or adapt an activity.

OUTPUT
Table:
Activity | Source | What Students Do | Fit With Goal | Time | License | Direct Link

Then:
- Rank the strongest 3.
- Explain what each adds to the unit.
- Note adaptation likely required.
- If no existing OER is strong enough, say: "No strong existing OER found."

Only after that, optionally suggest what kind of activity I could create or adapt myself.
Gap Search 2

Find Media, Data, or a Primary Source

Use this when students need something concrete to analyze: a speech, image, map, dataset, historical document, government report, video, recording, or other source material.

🌱 Quick Prompt — Keep It Simple

Best when you want a few source materials rather than another explanatory reading.

Keep this simple and focused.

I teach [COURSE].

For this unit, students are studying:
[TOPIC]

I want students to analyze a:
[PRIMARY SOURCE / VIDEO / IMAGE / SPEECH / MAP / DATASET / GOVERNMENT DOCUMENT / OTHER]

The source should help students:
[LEARNING GOAL]

Find 3–5 strong options that students can access at no cost.

Prioritize public-domain or openly licensed sources from credible institutions such as libraries, archives, museums, universities, government agencies, or established OER collections.

For each option, give me:
- Title or description
- Creator or source institution
- Date, when relevant
- Why it fits
- Rights or license information
- Direct link to the original source

Do not assume something is public domain or openly licensed.

If rights information is unclear, label it "Rights need verification."
🚀 Deeper Workflow — Search Across Collections

Best when provenance, rights, context, and teaching usefulness all matter.

Conduct a targeted search for openly usable media or primary-source material for this community college unit.

COURSE
[COURSE]

TOPIC
[TOPIC]

SOURCE TYPE
[PRIMARY DOCUMENT / VIDEO / IMAGE / AUDIO / SPEECH / DATASET / MAP / GOVERNMENT REPORT / OPEN]

LEARNING PURPOSE
Students should use the source to:
[ANALYZE / COMPARE / INTERPRET / APPLY / QUESTION / OTHER]

SEARCH PRIORITIES
Search credible original collections such as:
- government archives and agencies
- university and library digital collections
- museums and cultural institutions
- public-domain collections
- discipline-specific OER repositories
- other authoritative open collections

For each candidate, verify when possible:
- creator or issuing body
- date and historical/contextual information
- original hosting institution
- rights statement, public-domain status, or open license
- accessibility information such as captions, transcripts, alt text, or machine-readable data when relevant

Do not infer rights from age or from the fact that the source is visible online.

OUTPUT
Table:
Source | Institution | Why It Fits | Rights/License | Accessibility Notes | Direct Link | Verification Notes

Then:
1. Recommend the strongest 3.
2. Explain what students could analyze in each.
3. Flag context students would need before using the source.
4. Clearly identify any rights or accessibility question I must verify myself.
Gap Search 3

Add a Missing Perspective, Example, or Context

Use this when a unit technically covers the topic but still feels narrow, dated, abstract, or disconnected from the students in front of you.

🌱 Quick Prompt — Keep It Simple

Best when you can describe what feels missing without needing a complicated diversity audit.

Keep this simple and focused.

I teach [COURSE].

My current unit is about:
[TOPIC]

What I already use:
[BRIEF DESCRIPTION OF CURRENT MATERIAL]

The unit feels like it may be missing:
[A DIFFERENT PERSPECTIVE / A MORE CURRENT EXAMPLE / A LOCAL OR CALIFORNIA EXAMPLE / GLOBAL CONTEXT / A DIFFERENT TYPE OF VOICE / OTHER]

Help me find 3–5 OER or public-domain resources that could broaden the unit.

For each one, tell me:
- What it adds
- Why it complements rather than simply repeats my current material
- Who created or published it
- Open license or public-domain status
- Direct link

Use evidence from the resource rather than making assumptions about an author's identity or perspective.

Do not invent resources, links, licenses, or demographic information.
🚀 Deeper Workflow — Broaden the Unit Deliberately

Best when you want the search grounded in what the current unit already includes and what it actually lacks.

Help me broaden this unit with openly licensed or public-domain material without tokenizing perspectives or adding resources just for the sake of variety.

COURSE
[COURSE]

UNIT
[TOPIC]

CURRENT MATERIALS
[PASTE TITLES, LINKS, AUTHORS, SUMMARIES, OR A SHORT DESCRIPTION]

WHAT I THINK MAY BE MISSING
[DESCRIBE THE GAP]

PHASE 1: CHECK MY ASSUMPTION
Based only on the evidence available in my current materials, briefly assess whether the gap I identified appears real, partial, or unclear.

Do not infer an author's race, ethnicity, gender, nationality, ideology, or lived experience unless the source explicitly establishes information relevant to the task.

PHASE 2: SEARCH
Find OER or public-domain resources that add meaningful:
- perspective
- disciplinary approach
- historical context
- contemporary example
- geographic context
- type of evidence or source
as appropriate to the gap.

PHASE 3: EVALUATE COMPLEMENTARITY
For each resource, explain:
- what it adds
- how it differs from what students already have
- how it could deepen the learning goal rather than simply increase reading volume

VERIFICATION
Verify the resource, original source, and license or public-domain status when possible.

OUTPUT
Table:
Resource | What It Adds | How It Complements Existing Material | License/Rights | Direct Link | Verification Notes

Then:
1. Rank the strongest 3 additions.
2. Recommend where each could fit in the unit.
3. Flag anything that risks redundancy or unnecessary workload.
4. Say if the current materials already provide more range than my initial description suggested.

Before You Add Another Resource

Filling a gap should make the course stronger, not just make it longer. Before adding something, ask whether it earns its place.

  • Check the original source and rights. Verify open licensing or public-domain status yourself.
  • Protect student workload. If you add something, consider whether another reading or activity can become optional or shorter.
  • Look for real instructional value. A new resource should add depth, practice, context, or perspective rather than repeat what students already have.
  • Test the student experience. Open the resource, check navigation and accessibility, and make sure students can actually use it easily.

What Next?

Diagnosis says you need a reading? Run a targeted OER search. Have the pieces? Map them into your weeks or units. Found a possibility? Evaluate it before you add it.
