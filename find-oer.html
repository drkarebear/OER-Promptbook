Find OER Without Spending Hours Searching

Tell AI what you teach and let it help with the first pass: searching trusted sources, organizing possibilities, and giving you a manageable shortlist to review.

  • ✓ Search more broadly
  • ✓ Start with fewer, stronger options
  • ✓ Verify before you adopt

New to AI? You Can Start Right Here.

Choose the job you want done, copy the Quick Prompt, and replace anything in [brackets] with information about your course.

Important: Something that is free to read online is not automatically OER. The prompts below tell AI to verify an open license or public-domain status and to say when it cannot verify one.

What Are You Trying to Find?

📚 A Full OER Textbook Start with a shortlist for the whole course. 🧩 OER for One Unit Find material for one topic without changing everything. ↔ Something Like My Current Book Search for open alternatives with similar coverage. 📄 Turn My COR or Syllabus Into an OER Search Identify what the course needs, then search for resources that fit. ✓ Verify a Candidate List Clean up AI-generated or collected leads before you spend time evaluating them.
Search 1

Find a Full OER Textbook

Use this when you want a strong starting point for an entire course rather than dozens of disconnected resources.

🌱 Quick Prompt — Keep It Simple

Best when you want a short list and do not want to manage a complicated search.

Keep this simple and focused.

I teach [COURSE NAME AND NUMBER] at a community college.

My course covers:
[PASTE A SHORT COURSE DESCRIPTION, MAIN TOPICS, OR LEARNING OUTCOMES]

Help me find high-quality Open Educational Resources (OER) that could work as a primary textbook or major course resource.

Give me no more than 5 strong options.

For each option, tell me:
- Title and author or organization
- What it covers
- Why it may fit my course
- The open license or public-domain status
- A direct link to the original resource

Prioritize resources from colleges, universities, government agencies, established OER repositories, and other credible educational organizations.

Do not call something OER just because it is free online. Verify the open license or public-domain status when possible.

Do not invent titles, links, licenses, or availability. If you cannot verify something, clearly say "Needs verification."

End with a short recommendation telling me which 2 resources I should review first and why.
🚀 Deeper Workflow — Search More Deeply

Best when you want a broader search, stronger comparison, and documented gaps.

Conduct a thorough, evidence-based search for OER that could serve as the primary course material for [COURSE NAME AND NUMBER] at a community college.

COURSE CONTEXT
[PASTE COURSE DESCRIPTION, COR LANGUAGE, LEARNING OUTCOMES, TOPICS, OR OTHER RELEVANT CONTEXT]

SEARCH STRATEGY
1. Translate the course information into 6–10 specific content needs or search criteria.
2. Search multiple credible OER sources rather than relying on one repository or a general web search.
3. Prioritize complete textbooks or substantial collections first, but include a modular option if no single resource provides strong coverage.
4. Try alternate disciplinary terminology when an initial search is weak.
5. Before concluding that no strong resource exists for a major need, run a second search using different keywords or related disciplines.

VERIFICATION
For every recommendation:
- Verify that the resource and link actually exist.
- Identify the specific open license or public-domain status from the original source when possible.
- Do not treat "free to access" as equivalent to OER.
- Distinguish verified information from inference.
- Mark anything you cannot verify as "Needs verification."

EVALUATION
Assess each serious candidate for:
- coverage of the course's major content needs
- likely community-college level and usability
- currency where currency matters
- organization and ease of assigning sections
- accessibility information that is publicly available
- availability of instructor resources or ancillaries, if any

OUTPUT
First, give me a brief "What This Course Needs" list.

Then create a comparison table for the 5–8 strongest candidates with:
Resource | Source/Repository | Coverage | Strengths | Likely Gaps | License | Direct Link | Verification Notes

After the table:
1. Rank the strongest 3 options.
2. Explain the tradeoffs among them.
3. Identify any course needs that remain poorly covered.
4. Suggest a focused second-stage search for those gaps.

Keep the analysis useful for a faculty adoption decision rather than simply producing a long catalog of resources.
Want AI to tailor the prompt first?

Add: “Before you search, ask me up to 3 questions only if the answers would materially improve the search. Then revise the prompt for my course and show me the revised version.”

Search 2

Find OER for One Unit, Week, or Topic

Use this when most of your course already works and you only need a reading, chapter, activity, video, primary source, or other resource for one part.

🌱 Quick Prompt — Keep It Simple

Best when you know the topic and just need a few realistic options.

Keep this simple and focused.

I teach [COURSE] at a community college.

I need an Open Educational Resource for this topic or unit:
[TOPIC OR UNIT]

Students should be able to:
[OPTIONAL: LEARNING GOAL OR WHAT STUDENTS NEED TO DO]

I am looking for:
[READING / CHAPTER / VIDEO / ACTIVITY / PRIMARY SOURCE / ANY GOOD OPTION]

Find 3–5 strong OER options.

For each one, give me:
- Title
- What it is
- Approximate length or time, if you can verify it
- Why it fits this unit
- Open license or public-domain status
- A direct link to the original source

Prioritize resources that are appropriate for community college students and easy to assign.

Do not invent resources, links, licenses, or length estimates. Clearly mark anything you cannot verify.
🚀 Deeper Workflow — Search More Deeply

Best when the topic is specific, interdisciplinary, or hard to cover well.

Help me conduct a focused OER search for one part of my course rather than replacing the whole course.

COURSE
[COURSE NAME AND LEVEL]

UNIT OR TOPIC
[TOPIC]

LEARNING PURPOSE
[WHAT STUDENTS SHOULD UNDERSTAND, ANALYZE, PRACTICE, OR CREATE]

RESOURCE CONSTRAINTS
- Preferred type: [READING / CHAPTER / VIDEO / ACTIVITY / PRIMARY SOURCE / OPEN]
- Maximum student time: [OPTIONAL, FOR EXAMPLE 30 MINUTES]
- Reading level or other needs: [OPTIONAL]
- Perspectives, examples, or populations I want represented: [OPTIONAL]

SEARCH INSTRUCTIONS
Search multiple credible OER repositories, institutional collections, government sources, and discipline-specific open collections when relevant. Use alternate terminology and related disciplines if the obvious search terms produce weak results.

For each candidate:
- verify the resource exists
- link to the original source
- verify the open license or public-domain status when possible
- distinguish OER from resources that are merely free to access
- do not invent a reading time, accessibility feature, license, or other detail

OUTPUT
Give me the 5 strongest candidates in a table:
Resource | Type | Course Fit | Approx. Student Time | License | Direct Link | Verification Notes

Then:
1. Recommend the strongest 2.
2. Explain what each would let students do in this unit.
3. Note any important perspectives or content still missing.
4. If the results are weak, tell me what search terms or adjacent disciplines you would try next.
Search 3

Find OER Similar to a Book You Already Use

Use this when you like the intellectual shape of your current course and want alternatives without starting from a blank page.

🌱 Quick Prompt — Keep It Simple

Best when you can provide the title or table of contents of your current book.

Keep this simple and focused.

I currently use:
[BOOK TITLE AND AUTHOR]

I teach:
[COURSE NAME]

I would like to find OER that covers similar material so I can reduce or eliminate textbook costs for students.

If you can reliably find the book's table of contents, use it only as a guide. If you cannot, ask me to paste the table of contents instead of guessing.

Find up to 5 OER options that have the closest useful overlap.

For each one, show:
- Title
- What it covers well
- Important topics from my current book that it may not cover
- Open license or public-domain status
- Direct link to the original resource

Do not claim that an OER is an exact replacement unless the evidence supports that.

Do not invent the current book's contents, OER titles, links, or licenses.

End by telling me which 2 resources seem most worth reviewing first.
🚀 Deeper Workflow — Search More Deeply

Best when you want a structured coverage map rather than a vague “similar books” list.

Help me identify OER alternatives to the commercial or non-open material I currently use.

CURRENT RESOURCE
Title/author: [TITLE AND AUTHOR]
Table of contents or major topics:
[PASTE THE TABLE OF CONTENTS OR MAJOR CONTENT AREAS]

COURSE
[COURSE NAME, LEVEL, AND OPTIONAL OUTCOMES]

TASK
1. Convert the current resource's contents into a concise coverage map.
2. Search multiple credible OER sources for resources that provide meaningful overlap.
3. Consider both:
   - one-resource replacements
   - a small combination of OER if no single resource is a strong match
4. Search for equivalent instructional coverage, not just titles with similar wording.

VERIFICATION
- Verify each OER exists and link to its original source.
- Verify the open license or public-domain status when possible.
- Do not describe something as OER merely because it is free online.
- Do not infer contents you cannot verify.
- Clearly label uncertain information.

OUTPUT
Create a comparison table:
OER Candidate | Strong Coverage | Partial Coverage | Missing Coverage | License | Direct Link | Notes

Then provide:
- Best single-resource option
- Best small-resource combination, if useful
- Topics for which no strong OER match was found
- A recommended next search for those gaps

Be candid if the OER options are substantially weaker than my current resource. The goal is to help me make an informed decision, not to force an OER replacement.
Search 4

Turn Your COR or Syllabus Into an OER Search Plan

Use this when you know what you teach but have not yet found the resources. AI first identifies the course's reading and resource needs, then searches for OER that could meet them. If you already have candidate readings and want to place them week by week, use Map OER Readings to Your Course instead.

🌱 Quick Prompt — Keep It Simple

Best when you have course materials to paste but do not yet know what OER to search for.

Keep this simple and focused.

I teach at a community college.

Here is information from my course:
[PASTE YOUR COR, COURSE DESCRIPTION, LEARNING OUTCOMES, SYLLABUS, WEEKLY TOPICS, OR UNIT LIST]

Help me turn this into a focused OER search.

STEP 1: WHAT DOES MY COURSE NEED?
Create a brief list of the major content, skills, readings, examples, or other resources the course appears to need. Do not invent requirements that are not supported by what I pasted.

STEP 2: SEARCH
Use that list to find up to 5 strong OER resources that could meet a substantial part of those needs. Prioritize credible OER repositories, colleges and universities, government sources, and established open publishers.

For each resource, tell me:
- Title
- Which course needs it could support
- Important needs it does not appear to cover
- Open license or public-domain status
- Direct link to the original resource

STEP 3: WHAT IS STILL MISSING?
End with a short list of course needs that still need another resource or a more focused search.

Important:
- Do not invent resources, links, licenses, or course requirements.
- Do not call something OER simply because it is free online.
- Verify the open license or public-domain status when possible.
- If you cannot verify something, write “Needs verification.”
- Keep the answer manageable. I want a search plan and shortlist, not a giant catalog.
🚀 Deeper Workflow — Search More Deeply

Best when you want a course-needs map, a deliberate search strategy, and a documented shortlist of what was and was not found.

Use the course materials below to build and execute a structured OER discovery workflow.

COURSE MATERIALS
[PASTE COR, COURSE DESCRIPTION, SLOS, OBJECTIVES, TOPICS, SYLLABUS, WEEKLY SCHEDULE, UNIT LIST, OR OTHER RELEVANT MATERIAL]

PHASE 1: BUILD A COURSE-NEEDS MAP
Translate the material I provided into a concise map of what I may need to locate. Separate, when supported by the course materials:
- essential content or concepts
- recurring skills or practices
- types of readings, examples, primary sources, media, or activities that appear useful
- major assignments or applications
- needs tied to particular units or weeks
- content that appears optional or instructor-specific

Do not invent requirements that are not supported by the material I provide. If the materials are ambiguous, label the inference rather than treating it as a requirement.

PHASE 2: TURN THE MAP INTO SEARCH CRITERIA
For each major need, identify useful search terms, alternate terminology, and related disciplinary language where appropriate. Combine overlapping needs so the search does not become unnecessarily repetitive.

PHASE 3: SEARCH
Search multiple credible OER repositories and original institutional, governmental, or open-publisher sources. Use the course-needs map to look for both:
- substantial resources that could cover multiple course needs
- targeted resources for important needs that broader texts miss

If an initial search for an important need is weak, try at least one materially different search strategy before calling it a gap.

PHASE 4: VERIFY
For each serious candidate:
- confirm the resource exists
- provide a direct link to the original source
- identify the open license or public-domain status from the source when possible
- distinguish verified facts from inference
- label any unverified detail “Needs verification”
- never treat “free online” as proof that something is OER

PHASE 5: REPORT
First, show a brief table:
Course Need | Search Terms/Strategy | What a Useful Resource Should Provide

Then show the strongest results in a second table:
Resource | Course Needs Covered | Important Gaps | Type | License | Direct Link | Verification Notes

Then provide:
1. The 3 strongest overall candidates and why they deserve faculty review.
2. Course needs that remain uncovered or weakly covered.
3. The next 3 focused searches you would run for those gaps.
4. Any places where one strong resource could replace several weaker or repetitive resources.

Keep the output decision-focused. The goal is to turn my existing course into a search strategy and a manageable shortlist, not to redesign my weekly schedule or bury me in a long catalog.
Search 5

Verify and Clean My Candidate List

Use this after an AI search, web search, colleague recommendation, or repository search has given you a messy list of possibilities. The goal is not to decide which OER is best yet. It is to remove bad leads, correct details, and send only real candidates forward for faculty review.

🌱 Quick Prompt

Best when you already have a short list and want to know which items are real OER candidates worth opening.

I have a list of possible OER resources. Help me verify and clean the list before I spend time evaluating them.

MY CANDIDATE LIST
[PASTE TITLES, LINKS, CITATIONS, OR AI-GENERATED RESULTS]

For each item:
- Confirm whether the resource appears to exist.
- Give me the correct title and author or organization, if the information I pasted is inaccurate.
- Link to the original or most authoritative source rather than a search result, mirror, or aggregator when possible.
- Identify the specific open license or public-domain status from the original source when possible.
- Tell me if it is only free to access rather than openly licensed.
- Flag broken links, duplicate entries, partial previews, outdated versions, or items you cannot verify.

Use one of these labels for each item:
- KEEP FOR SCREENING — the resource, source, and open status are sufficiently verified to deserve faculty review.
- NEEDS MANUAL VERIFICATION — the resource may be useful, but an important detail could not be confirmed.
- REMOVE AS DISCOVERY NOISE — the item is duplicated, broken, unverifiable, not actually OER, or otherwise not a useful lead.

Create a brief table:
Candidate | Status | Corrected Details | License/Open Status | Best Direct Link | Verification Note

Do not invent corrections, licenses, links, or verification. If you cannot open webpages or verify current information, say so clearly instead of pretending that you checked it.

Do not deeply evaluate course quality or make an adoption decision yet. I only want a clean, trustworthy candidate list for the next stage.
🚀 Deeper Workflow

Best when the list is long, came from several searches, or contains uncertain citations, mirrors, duplicates, and mixed free/OER materials.

Audit and clean the candidate list below before I begin a full OER evaluation.

CANDIDATE LIST
[PASTE TITLES, URLS, CITATIONS, REPOSITORY RESULTS, OR AI-GENERATED RECOMMENDATIONS]

PURPOSE
This is a discovery-quality audit, not an adoption decision. I want to know which candidates are real, correctly identified, openly licensed, and worth sending forward for deeper faculty review.

PHASE 1: RESOLVE EACH CANDIDATE
For every item:
1. Determine whether the resource actually exists.
2. Identify the canonical title, author/creator or organization, and edition/version when available.
3. Find the original or most authoritative landing page. Prefer the publisher, authoring institution, government agency, or established OER repository over mirrors and search-result pages.
4. Identify the exact open license or public-domain status from the source itself when possible.
5. Distinguish among:
   - verified OER/openly licensed
   - verified public domain
   - free to access but open license not verified
   - access restricted, preview only, or paywalled
   - unverifiable or apparently nonexistent
6. Check for duplicate records, alternate URLs for the same work, superseded editions, and obvious citation errors.

PHASE 2: CLEAN THE LIST
Assign exactly one status:
- KEEP FOR SCREENING — sufficiently verified as a real open/public-domain candidate.
- NEEDS MANUAL VERIFICATION — plausible candidate, but a material fact remains uncertain.
- REMOVE AS DISCOVERY NOISE — duplicate, broken, fabricated/unverifiable, preview-only, not actually open, or clearly not the resource represented in the original list.

Do not remove something merely because it may be imperfect for my course. Course fit belongs in the evaluation stage.

PHASE 3: REPORT
Create a table:
Original Candidate | Clean Status | Canonical Resource | Creator/Organization | License/Open Status | Best Direct Link | What You Verified | What Still Needs Checking

Then provide:
1. A deduplicated list of only the KEEP FOR SCREENING candidates.
2. A short manual-check list for NEEDS MANUAL VERIFICATION items.
3. A brief explanation for anything removed as discovery noise.
4. Any patterns in the bad leads, such as repeated mirrors, hallucinated citations, confusingly similar titles, or resources that are free but not OER.

VERIFICATION RULES
- Never infer an open license from free access.
- Do not invent titles, authors, URLs, editions, or license information.
- Prefer evidence from the original source.
- If your current AI environment cannot browse, open links, or verify current webpages, state that limitation and convert uncertain claims into a manual-check list instead of presenting them as verified.
- Keep verified facts separate from inference.

Stop at discovery cleanup. Do not rank the resources by quality or recommend adoption unless I ask for that in a separate step.
Why do this before evaluating?

It prevents you from spending faculty review time on hallucinated citations, dead links, duplicate records, previews, or materials that are free online but are not actually OER.

Before You Decide

AI can save you search time, but it should not make the adoption decision for you. Open the original resource and check the things that matter for your course.

  • Verify the license. Free access alone does not make a resource OER.
  • Open the links. AI can produce outdated, incorrect, or invented citations.
  • Check the actual content. Review accuracy, depth, organization, currency, and course fit yourself.
  • Check accessibility. Do not assume a resource is accessible because AI says it is.

What Next?

Have a messy candidate list? Verify and clean it first. Candidates verified? Evaluate the OER. Already have candidate readings? Map them to your course week by week. Almost a fit? Find OER to fill the gaps. No strong OER? Explore other ZTC options.
