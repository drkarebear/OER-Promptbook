AI Prompts for OER/ZTC Liaisons

Use AI to reduce the sorting, planning, and drafting work around campus OER and Zero Textbook Cost efforts, while keeping faculty choice, local policy, accessibility, funding, and implementation decisions in human hands.

AI Can Help Organize the Work. It Should Not Run the Program.

These prompts are designed for first-pass analysis, planning, outreach, and communication. They can help you spot patterns, prepare conversations, and turn scattered information into a manageable next step.

Verify anything that affects local policy, funding, course designation, accessibility, licensing, faculty workload, or student access. Faculty still choose their course materials, and local OER/ZTC professionals still make program decisions.

What Are You Trying to Do?

💬 Prepare for a Faculty Conversation Turn a broad request or concern into a useful, low-pressure conversation. 🔎 Identify Promising Opportunities Find courses or needs where OER/ZTC support may have the most value. 🧭 Map a ZTC Pathway Organize known course options and identify the gaps that still need work. ✓ Prioritize Campus Efforts Sort a long list of possible projects into a realistic action plan. ✉ Draft Faculty Outreach Write invitations and follow-ups that are useful rather than salesy.
Liaison 1

Prepare for a Faculty OER/ZTC Conversation

Use this when a faculty member is interested, hesitant, overwhelmed, or simply asking what options exist. The goal is not to persuade them into a predetermined choice. It is to make the next conversation more useful.

🌱 Quick Prompt

Best when you have an email, note, or short description of the situation.

I support OER and Zero Textbook Cost work at a college.

Here is the faculty situation:
[PASTE THE EMAIL, REQUEST, OR BRIEF DESCRIPTION]

Help me prepare for a supportive faculty conversation.

Identify:
1. What the faculty member appears to need or care about
2. Any practical concern they may be signaling, such as time, quality, ancillaries, accessibility, course fit, or uncertainty about where to start
3. The 2-3 questions most worth asking next
4. Resources or kinds of support I could offer without overwhelming them
5. A reasonable next step that preserves faculty choice

Do not assume the faculty member is resistant to OER.
Do not frame OER as automatically better than every other option.
Do not invent local funding, policy, library access, or available resources.

End with a short conversation checklist.
🚀 Deeper Workflow

Best when the conversation involves several barriers or a more complicated course context.

Act as a planning assistant for an OER/ZTC liaison preparing to support a faculty member.

FACULTY CONTEXT
[PASTE REQUEST, NOTES, COURSE INFORMATION, OR OTHER CONTEXT]

LOCAL SUPPORT I KNOW IS AVAILABLE
[OPTIONAL: LIBRARY HELP, OER GRANT, DESIGN SUPPORT, DEPARTMENT SUPPORT, ETC.]

PHASE 1 - UNDERSTAND THE NEED
Separate:
- the instructional goal
- the current material or access problem
- faculty concerns
- student access concerns
- workload or time concerns
- questions that are still unknown

PHASE 2 - AVOID ASSUMPTIONS
Flag anything that would require local verification, including:
- funding
- policy
- course designation
- library licensing
- accessibility
- staffing
- deadlines

PHASE 3 - PLAN THE CONVERSATION
Give me no more than 4 useful questions to ask.
Then suggest 2-3 kinds of support that fit the situation.

PHASE 4 - NEXT STEP
Recommend the smallest useful next action. Prefer something manageable, such as reviewing one course need, identifying 3-5 possible resources, or connecting the faculty member with the right person.

Do not pressure the faculty member to adopt OER.
Do not invent local resources.

OUTPUT
A. What seems to matter most
B. Questions to ask
C. Support I could offer
D. What I need to verify
E. Smallest useful next step
Liaison 2

Identify Promising OER/ZTC Opportunities

Use this when you have a list of courses, costs, enrollments, faculty requests, or other non-sensitive program information and need a first-pass way to see where support might have the greatest payoff.

🌱 Quick Prompt

Best for turning a messy list into a shortlist worth human review.

I support OER/ZTC work at a college.

Here is a list of courses or program information:
[PASTE NON-SENSITIVE COURSE DATA OR NOTES]

Help me identify a manageable shortlist of promising OER/ZTC support opportunities.

Consider only information actually present in my data, such as:
- enrollment or number of sections
- current material cost
- repeated faculty requests
- known access problems
- existing OER or library-supported possibilities
- courses that may affect a larger pathway

Create a table:
Course/Area | Why It May Be Worth Exploring | Evidence From My Data | What I Still Need to Learn

Then identify 3-5 items that appear most worth a human follow-up.

Do not rank faculty members.
Do not infer faculty willingness.
Do not invent textbook costs, enrollment, adoption status, or available OER.
🚀 Deeper Workflow

Best when you want transparent criteria rather than a vague AI ranking.

Act as an analysis assistant for a college OER/ZTC liaison.

DATA
[PASTE NON-SENSITIVE COURSE, COST, ENROLLMENT, PATHWAY, OR PROGRAM INFORMATION]

MY CURRENT PRIORITIES
[OPTIONAL: STUDENT SAVINGS, HIGH-ENROLLMENT COURSES, PATHWAY COMPLETION, FACULTY SUPPORT, ACCESS GAPS, ETC.]

STEP 1 - CLEAN THE INPUT
List the fields actually available in my data.
Identify important information that is missing.

STEP 2 - CREATE TRANSPARENT CRITERIA
Using only my stated priorities and available data, propose 3-5 criteria for identifying opportunities.
Explain why each criterion matters.
Do not assign hidden weights.

STEP 3 - APPLY THE CRITERIA
Create a table:
Course/Area | Evidence | Relevant Criteria | Potential Value | Unknowns | Follow-Up Needed

STEP 4 - SHORTLIST
Identify no more than 5 promising opportunities for human review.
For each, explain the evidence behind the suggestion.

STEP 5 - CHECK THE LIMITS
List reasons the shortlist could change after talking with faculty, librarians, disability/accessibility staff, administrators, or students.

Do not infer faculty willingness, quality of a resource, or local feasibility from data that does not establish it.
Keep the shortlist about opportunities, not people.

A course can be promising for OER/ZTC support without implying that its current instructor is doing anything wrong or is ready to change materials.

Liaison 3

Map a ZTC Pathway and Find the Remaining Gaps

Use this when you are looking across a degree, certificate, transfer pattern, or other sequence and need to distinguish known ZTC options from unresolved gaps.

🌱 Quick Prompt

Best when you already have a course list and some known ZTC information.

I am reviewing this program or course sequence for possible ZTC pathway planning.

PROGRAM / PATHWAY
[PASTE COURSE REQUIREMENTS OR SEQUENCE]

KNOWN ZTC INFORMATION
[PASTE WHAT IS ALREADY CONFIRMED]

Organize this into a pathway table:
Requirement | Course/Choice | Confirmed ZTC? | Evidence I Provided | Gap or Question | Next Check

Then identify:
1. Courses already covered by confirmed ZTC options
2. Courses where I need more information
3. Courses that appear to be the most important remaining gaps
4. Cases where multiple course choices make the pathway more complicated

Use only the information I provide.
Do not label a course ZTC unless my information confirms it.
Do not invent scheduling, section availability, course rotations, or faculty adoption.
🚀 Deeper Workflow

Best when electives, multiple pathways, or uncertain course status make the map harder to interpret.

Act as a planning assistant for an OER/ZTC liaison mapping a possible ZTC pathway.

PROGRAM REQUIREMENTS
[PASTE CATALOG OR PROGRAM REQUIREMENTS]

CONFIRMED LOCAL ZTC INFORMATION
[PASTE CONFIRMED COURSE/SECTION/RESOURCE INFORMATION]

OTHER VERIFIED CONTEXT
[OPTIONAL: COURSE ROTATION, ENROLLMENT, SEQUENCING, LOCAL DEFINITIONS]

PHASE 1 - MAP THE REQUIREMENTS
Separate:
- required courses
- choose-one groups
- electives
- prerequisites or sequencing information that I actually provided

PHASE 2 - APPLY ONLY CONFIRMED ZTC INFORMATION
For every requirement, mark:
- Confirmed ZTC
- Not confirmed
- More information needed

PHASE 3 - FIND GAPS
Identify where a student could still encounter a non-ZTC requirement or an uncertain choice.
Distinguish:
- a single-course gap
- an elective-choice gap
- an information gap
- an implementation gap

PHASE 4 - PRIORITIZE FOLLOW-UP
Suggest a practical order for investigating the gaps, based only on the evidence I provided.

OUTPUT
A. Pathway map
B. Confirmed coverage
C. Remaining gaps
D. Questions requiring local verification
E. 3-5 next actions

Do not claim the pathway is fully ZTC unless the supplied information actually establishes that.
Liaison 4

Turn a Long OER/ZTC Wish List Into a Realistic Action Plan

Use this when your campus has more possible projects than time. AI can help sort the work, but priorities should come from stated local goals and verified constraints.

🌱 Quick Prompt

Best when you need a short now-next-later plan.

I support OER/ZTC work at a college and have more possible projects than I can do at once.

POSSIBLE PROJECTS
[PASTE LIST]

KNOWN DEADLINES OR CONSTRAINTS
[PASTE IF RELEVANT]

MY STATED PRIORITIES
[PASTE IF RELEVANT]

Organize the projects into:
DO NOW
DO NEXT
LATER / WHEN CAPACITY ALLOWS
NEEDS MORE INFORMATION

For each item, give a one-sentence reason based only on the information I supplied.

Also identify:
- quick wins
- projects with dependencies
- projects that may require collaboration before work begins
- anything that should not be prioritized without more evidence

Do not invent deadlines, staffing, funding, authority, or campus priorities.
🚀 Deeper Workflow

Best when you are balancing impact, effort, dependencies, and limited capacity.

Act as a project-planning assistant for a college OER/ZTC liaison.

PROJECT LIST
[PASTE]

LOCAL GOALS
[PASTE]

KNOWN CAPACITY / DEADLINES / CONSTRAINTS
[PASTE]

COLLABORATORS OR DEPENDENCIES I KNOW ABOUT
[PASTE]

STEP 1 - NORMALIZE THE LIST
Turn each item into a clear project or next action.
Flag items that are too vague to prioritize.

STEP 2 - USE TRANSPARENT FACTORS
Compare projects using only factors supported by my input, such as:
- potential reach
- urgency
- student access impact
- effort
- dependency on another person or office
- readiness
- connection to a stated local goal

STEP 3 - BUILD A PLAN
Create a table:
Project | Why It Matters | Effort/Dependency Evidence | Readiness | Suggested Timing | Next Action

STEP 4 - PROTECT CAPACITY
Recommend no more than 3 immediate priorities.
Move the rest into a visible queue rather than pretending everything can happen at once.

STEP 5 - FLAG HUMAN DECISIONS
Identify any item that needs leadership, faculty, library, accessibility, curriculum, or funding input before it can move forward.

Do not invent authority, staffing, timelines, or outcomes.
Liaison 5

Draft Faculty Outreach That Respects Faculty Choice

Use this for invitations, follow-ups, or targeted outreach. The strongest messages usually offer concrete help and a manageable first step rather than asking faculty to “switch to OER.”

🌱 Quick Prompt

Best for a short email that sounds like support rather than a campaign.

I support OER/ZTC work at a college.

AUDIENCE
[ONE FACULTY MEMBER / DEPARTMENT / PROGRAM / GROUP]

WHY I AM REACHING OUT
[PASTE THE REAL REASON]

SUPPORT I CAN ACTUALLY OFFER
[PASTE VERIFIED SUPPORT]

Draft a brief, collegial outreach email.

It should:
- explain why I am contacting them
- connect the message to a practical faculty or student need
- make clear that participation or material choice remains theirs
- offer one specific kind of help
- give one easy next step
- avoid guilt, pressure, or exaggerated claims about OER

Do not add grants, stipends, services, deadlines, or policies that I did not provide.

Keep it concise and human.
🚀 Deeper Workflow

Best when outreach needs to be tailored to a specific department or situation.

I am an OER/ZTC liaison preparing faculty outreach.

AUDIENCE
[DEPARTMENT, PROGRAM, FACULTY GROUP, OR INDIVIDUAL]

CONTEXT
[WHY THIS OUTREACH MAKES SENSE NOW]

VERIFIED LOCAL SUPPORT
[LIBRARY HELP, SEARCH SUPPORT, WORKSHOP, FUNDING, DESIGN HELP, ETC.]

KNOWN CONCERNS OR CONSTRAINTS
[OPTIONAL]

Create two versions:

VERSION A - VERY SHORT
75-120 words for a busy faculty audience.

VERSION B - SLIGHTLY MORE CONTEXT
150-220 words for a department or program conversation.

Both versions should:
1. Lead with the practical reason for the message.
2. Avoid implying that current faculty choices are a problem.
3. Describe OER/ZTC as an option or support opportunity, not a mandate.
4. Name only resources and support I actually provided.
5. Offer one low-effort next step.
6. Avoid generic claims such as “OER improves student success” unless I supplied evidence for that claim.

Then give me 3 possible subject lines that are specific and not promotional.
