The OER Promptbook

A practical collection of AI prompts designed to make OER and zero-textbook-cost work a little easier for community college educators.

Why This Exists

Finding and adopting zero-cost course materials can save students money, but the process can also take a great deal of faculty and staff time. Searching across repositories, comparing resources, finding alternatives, checking access, and figuring out how new materials fit an existing course can become a project of its own.

The OER Promptbook is designed to help with some of that first-pass work. The prompts use AI to help narrow searches, organize possibilities, compare information, and create starting points for decisions that still belong to faculty, librarians, and OER/ZTC professionals.

About the Project

The OER Promptbook is an independent faculty project created and maintained by Karen Crozer. It is designed to help faculty and OER practitioners use AI thoughtfully to find, evaluate, compare, and plan with open and zero-cost course materials.

I maintain the project on a volunteer basis. It will continue to evolve through corrections, shared prompts, feedback, and the experiences of educators using these tools in their own courses.

The Approach

Save time

Use AI for the searching, sorting, and first-pass comparison work that can otherwise consume hours.

Keep professional judgment human

AI can surface possibilities. People still verify sources, licensing, accessibility, quality, and course fit.

Work with what already exists

Moving toward OER or ZTC does not have to mean rebuilding a course from the ground up.

Author’s Note on AI Use

The prompting strategies in this project grew out of my own experience using AI-assisted searches to locate OER and ZTC materials as I converted six courses to zero-textbook-cost or open educational resource models.

I later used GPT-5.6 to help refine those prompts, organize the guidance, and make the process more intuitive and easier to follow. I reviewed, revised, and made the final decisions about what to include based on my experience as an educator and OER adopter.

AI helped me make the process clearer and more usable. The search strategy, instructional judgment, and final recommendations remain my own.

Use the Promptbook Beside Trusted OER Collections

The Promptbook is designed to work with resource lists you already trust. For California Community College faculty, the ASCCC OERI OER collections by discipline are a useful starting point. You can bring titles and links from those collections into the Promptbook prompts rather than asking AI to search from scratch.

If you have a resource list saved as a PDF, spreadsheet, document, or other file, you can also drag that file into an AI chat when the tool supports uploads. The prompt can then work from the material you provided.

Accessibility

I aim to make The OER Promptbook usable by as many people as possible and to meet or exceed WCAG 2.2 Level AA where the project itself controls the experience. The site uses semantic headings and landmarks, keyboard-accessible navigation, visible focus indicators, high-contrast text and controls, responsive reflow, large interaction targets, and reduced-motion support.

Third-party services linked or embedded here, including Google Forms, Padlet, and external AI tools, are maintained by their own providers and may have different accessibility features or barriers. The Padlet showcase is not loaded until a visitor chooses to load it, and a direct-link alternative is provided.

I use WCAG 2.2 Level AA as the project target and test for common WAVE errors, contrast errors, keyboard barriers, reflow problems, and accessible-name issues. Automated tools cannot certify legal compliance, so human review and assistive-technology testing remain part of the accessibility process.

If you encounter an accessibility barrier in the Promptbook itself, please email me about the accessibility issue and, if possible, include the page and a short description of what was difficult to use.

Have a Prompt or Idea to Add?

I maintain this project on a volunteer basis, and I would love for it to improve through the experience of people doing this work across community colleges.

If you have a prompt that has worked well, a workflow that would be useful to add, or an idea other educators might want to try, share it through the Community Prompt Lab. Community submissions are kept separate from the maintained core Promptbook so people can experiment, adapt, and verify what works for them.
