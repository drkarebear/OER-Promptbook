The OER Promptbook

A practical collection of AI prompts designed to make OER and zero-textbook-cost work a little easier for community college educators.

Why This Exists

Finding and adopting zero-cost course materials can save students money, but the process can also take a great deal of faculty and staff time. Searching across repositories, comparing resources, finding alternatives, checking access, and figuring out how new materials fit an existing course can become a project of its own.

The OER Promptbook is designed to help with some of that first-pass work. The prompts use AI to help narrow searches, organize possibilities, compare information, and create starting points for decisions that still belong to faculty, librarians, and OER/ZTC professionals.

About the Project

The OER Promptbook is an independent faculty project created and maintained by Karen Crozer. It is designed to help faculty and OER practitioners use AI thoughtfully to find, evaluate, compare, and plan with open and zero-cost course materials.

I maintain the project on a volunteer basis. It will continue to evolve through corrections, shared prompts, feedback, and the experiences of educators using these tools in their own courses.

The Approach

Save time

Use AI for the searching, sorting, and first-pass comparison work that can otherwise consume hours.

Keep professional judgment human

AI can surface possibilities. People still verify sources, licensing, accessibility, quality, and course fit.

Work with what already exists

Moving toward OER or ZTC does not have to mean rebuilding a course from the ground up.

Author’s Note on AI Use

The prompting strategies in this project grew out of my own experience using AI-assisted searches to locate OER and ZTC materials as I converted six courses to zero-textbook-cost or open educational resource models.

I later used GPT-5.6 to help refine those prompts, organize the guidance, and make the process more intuitive and easier to follow. I reviewed, revised, and made the final decisions about what to include based on my experience as an educator and OER adopter.

AI helped me make the process clearer and more usable. The search strategy, instructional judgment, and final recommendations remain my own.
